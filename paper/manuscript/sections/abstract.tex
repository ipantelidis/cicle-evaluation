\begin{abstract}
Narrowing the label set before prompting a large language model (LLM) is a cheap way to help in-context text classification.
Conformal In-Context Learning (CICLe) does this with a small classifier calibrated by class-conditional conformal prediction.
Earlier evidence for it rested on a protocol with unlisted labels, unequal example pools and unseeded conformal sets.
We correct the protocol and evaluate CICLe on five English datasets, six LLMs with 3B to 8B parameters and three seeds, with paired bootstrap tests.
Narrowing gives a small but reliable gain over few-shot prompting, 0.5 to 1.8 macro-F1 points, with up to 38\% shorter prompts. % CHECK
How one narrows matters.
When the labelled pool is long-tailed, class-conditional conformal sets keep their coverage, while top-$k$ and probability-mass sets lose 4 to 11 points. % CHECK
The gain is largest when label names are uninformative.
With 1,600 balanced labels a fine-tuned encoder wins. LLM pipelines pay off when the pool is skewed.
\end{abstract}
