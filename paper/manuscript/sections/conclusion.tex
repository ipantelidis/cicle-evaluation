\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We evaluated conformal label narrowing for in-context text classification under a protocol that lists the admissible labels in every prompt, retrieves from one labelled pool for every method and pairs runs by seed.
Four rules of thumb follow.
First, compare prompting methods only with the label list and the example pool held equal and invalid outputs reported, or the comparison measures the prompt format.
Second, narrowing with CICLe is worth 0.5 to 1.8 macro-F1 points over few-shot prompting on short texts and 5 points on long documents with Per-Class retrieval, and it shortens Per-Class prompts by 11 to 45\%. Third, once the labelled pool is long-tailed, class-conditional conformal sets keep their coverage on rare classes and top-$m$, probability-mass and marginal conformal sets do not, and the LLM cannot recover a label it was not offered. On balanced data any rule will do, and narrowing helps most when label names carry no meaning.
Fourth, with a plentiful balanced pool, a fine-tuned encoder, or the base classifier alone when it is strong, beats every 3B to 8B pipeline. With a skewed or small pool the in-context pipelines degrade far less, and class-conditional narrowing is the rule to use once a few hundred calibration examples exist. A looser miscoverage level pays where the classifier is strong.
Finally, class imbalance does not predict CICLe's benefit over few-shot prompting. It separates narrowing rules from each other.
