\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Overall coverage usually tracks what a narrowing rule costs downstream, but on naturally long-tailed data with many classes it can hide classes that a rule never offers. Rule: report per-class coverage next to overall coverage.

At matched set size the narrowing rule matters under a skewed labelled pool and not on balanced data, consistently across models and seeds, and under a 100-fold skew the wrong rule costs several times what narrowing gains over few-shot prompting. Rule: calibrate per class whenever the labelled pool is skewed.

The LLM almost never recovers a label that is missing from the candidate set, so the set is a hard constraint. Rule: choose $\alpha$ with the rare classes in mind, since their coverage bounds macro-F1.

Narrowing gains less than 2 points over few-shot prompting on short texts, not consistently across models and seeds everywhere, more when label names carry no meaning, and saves input tokens with Per-Class retrieval. For the encoder recipes we tested, fine-tuning wins on a plentiful balanced pool. Rule: use a class-conditionally narrowed prompt when labels are skewed or scarce, and fine-tune when they are plentiful and balanced.
