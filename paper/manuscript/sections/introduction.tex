\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Small open large language models (LLMs) can classify text from a few labelled examples placed in the prompt \citep{brown2020language}, but real label spaces make this hard.
Accuracy depends on which examples are shown and in what order \citep{liu2022makes,lu2022fantastically,zhao2021calibrate}, with many classes the prompt must carry a long label list, and with a skewed labelled pool the rare classes have few examples to show.
A common remedy is to let a small classifier trained on the labelled pool narrow the label space first, so that the LLM chooses among a few candidates \citep{milios2023incontext,randl2024cicle}.
Narrowing has a cost that overall numbers hide.
A label left out of the candidate set cannot be predicted, and a set that contains the true label for 95\% of the test instances can still leave out a rare class almost every time.

This paper asks how much the narrowing rule matters for the final prediction once prompt content, retrieval and candidate-set size are held fixed, and under which data conditions.
We compare the class-conditional conformal sets of Conformal In-Context Learning (CICLe) \citep{randl2024cicle} with top-$m$ sets of the same mean size and with probability-mass and marginal conformal sets, on labelled pools whose imbalance we control and on naturally long-tailed datasets, with five English datasets and six open LLMs of 3B to 8B parameters. % PENDING: six datasets with MASSIVE; size-matched probability-mass and marginal rules

Conformal theory already says that class-conditional calibration gives coverage within every class \citep{vovk2012conditional}.
What is not known is whether and when this matters for an LLM that chooses from the set, and that is what we measure.
A size-matched rule with higher overall coverage can still lose macro-F1, as top-$m$ sets do on GoEmotions, where they cover more test instances than CICLe's sets and lose about 2.5 points.
Marginal calibration is level with class-conditional calibration on balanced data and collapses under a skewed pool. % PENDING: size-matched marginal and probability-mass rules on the naturally long-tailed datasets
Under a 100-fold skew the loss from the wrong rule is several times larger than the gain of CICLe over few-shot prompting.
Our contributions follow the order of the Results.
\begin{enumerate}
  \setlength\itemsep{0pt}
  \item \textbf{Overall coverage hides unreachable classes.} Rules with similar overall coverage differ sharply in which classes they keep (Section~\ref{sec:res-coverage}).
  \item \textbf{The rule matters under skew, not on balanced data.} At matched set size, top-$m$ sets lose 3 to 7 points to class-conditional sets under a 100-fold skew and nothing measurable on balanced data (Section~\ref{sec:res-narrowing}).
  \item \textbf{The LLM does not recover a label left out.} It answers correctly for 0.9\% of the instances whose gold label the set misses, so per-class coverage, not overall coverage, bounds macro-F1 (Section~\ref{sec:res-consequence}). % PENDING: class-averaged coverage against macro-F1 analysis
  \item \textbf{When narrowing pays off.} Narrowing gains 0.5 to 1.8 points over few-shot prompting on short texts and more when label names carry no meaning, while fine-tuned encoders and the choice of $\alpha$ give qualified, dataset-dependent advice (Sections~\ref{sec:res-gain} and~\ref{sec:res-secondary}).
\end{enumerate}
Section~\ref{sec:setup} also motivates a protocol choice that the comparison needs, holding the label information in the prompt constant across methods.
