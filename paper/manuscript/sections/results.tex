\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All numbers are at the reference setting and averaged over the six small models and three seeds unless stated.
A gain is CICLe minus an alternative in macro-F1 points, with a 95\% paired bootstrap interval where it is the key number.

\subsection{Comparisons Must Hold the Label Information Constant}
\label{sec:res-protocol}

A comparison of prompting methods must hold the label information constant, because prompts that show few labels produce unparseable outputs that decide the comparison.
The prompt format of the released CICLe implementation lists no labels, which suits its Per-Class retrieval, and we ran it with Fixed retrieval, which shows only the retrieved labels, on seed 42 (Table~\ref{tab:original}, Appendix~\ref{app:original}).
Up to 65\% of a model's few-shot outputs then matched no label, and the per-model effect of narrowing changed sign between the two formats in a third of the cells. % PENDING: MASSIVE original-prompt rerun; label-list ablation (only the list removed, same parser and token budget; every dataset, seed 42, Fixed and Per-Class, k in {1,4}) will replace the original-format comparison as the main evidence and separate the list from the parser and the 5-token cap
Under the corrected protocol Per-Class CICLe never significantly hurts any of the six models (Table~\ref{tab:per_model}, Appendix~\ref{app:models}), which leaves the question of how large its gain is.

\subsection{Narrowing Gives a Small Gain and a Shorter Prompt}
\label{sec:res-gain}

Narrowing the label set gives a small, reliable gain over few-shot prompting and a shorter prompt.
Table~\ref{tab:main} compares zero-shot, few-shot and CICLe prompting at $k = 4$ on the six datasets, with the paired gain pooled over all $k$ in panel~B, and Figure~\ref{fig:main_tokens} plots every configuration against its prompt length.
CICLe is ahead in nine of ten dataset and retrieval cells, with gains between 0.5 and 1.8 points whose intervals exclude zero (Table~\ref{tab:main}B). % PENDING: MASSIVE column of Table 2 changes the cell count and the range
The exception is Fixed retrieval on Yahoo Answers, where one retrieved example already carries the label and the difference is not significant.
The gains do not grow with $k$ (Appendix~\ref{app:full}).
The one large gain is Ohsumed with Per-Class retrieval, $+5.2$ [$+4.4$, $+6.1$] points, where a few-shot prompt holds one long abstract for every class and the LLM loses the input among them, while CICLe halves the list.
The candidate list alone, with no examples, is worth about one retrieved example (Table~\ref{tab:main}A, row $k = 0$).

The saving is on the prompt.
Per-Class retrieval places $k$ examples per admissible class, so a narrower list shortens the prompt in proportion, by 11 to 45\% at equal $k$ (Table~\ref{tab:main}B) % PENDING: MASSIVE
, at the same or higher macro-F1, which is why the filled blue markers of Figure~\ref{fig:main_tokens} lie above and to the left of the grey ones.
Fixed retrieval is cheaper still, and on GoEmotions it also scores higher, because examples for most of its 28 classes dilute the prompt more than they inform it.
If narrowing helps, the next question is whether the rule used to narrow matters.

\subsection{How You Narrow Matters}
\label{sec:res-narrowing}

How one narrows matters only when the labelled pool is long-tailed.
Figure~\ref{fig:main_narrowing} compares CICLe with top-$m$, probability-mass and marginal conformal sets built from the same classifier at the same mean size on Yahoo Answers and SST-5, whose labelled pool is resampled to a 10 or 100 times imbalance while the test sample stays fixed, and Table~\ref{tab:narrowing} (Appendix~\ref{app:narrowing}) gives every comparison.
On balanced data the four rules are interchangeable, and no difference on SST-5 or SemEval-18 exceeds one point (Table~\ref{tab:narrowing}, SST-5 and SemEval-18 rows). % PENDING: marginal CP on SemEval-18
Under imbalance the conformal sets keep their coverage while the other rules lose it on the rare classes (Figure~\ref{fig:main_narrowing}, right), and an LLM cannot recover a label it is not offered.
At 100 times imbalance CICLe beats the alternatives by 4 to 13 points (Table~\ref{tab:narrowing}, 100$\times$ rows), the marginal rule being worst because its shared threshold is set by the confident head classes.


The naturally long-tailed datasets show the same effect at smaller size. % PENDING: MASSIVE, the second naturally long-tailed dataset
On GoEmotions and Ohsumed CICLe is one to three points ahead of every alternative, and the lowest per-class coverage of every alternative there is zero (Table~\ref{tab:set_stats}).
Two things do not happen.
CICLe's margin over few-shot prompting does not grow with imbalance (Figure~\ref{fig:main_narrowing}, left, grey line), because few-shot prompting lists every label and cannot lose coverage.
And narrowing is not free of harm.
When the set misses the gold label, which the $1 - \alpha$ coverage bounds, CICLe can only break an answer that few-shot prompting had right (Table~\ref{tab:fixes}, Appendix~\ref{app:instances}).
An oracle set that always contains the gold label shows that on balanced data the remaining room is in ranking, not in coverage (Table~\ref{tab:narrowing}, oracle column).
So far the LLM has seen label names it understands, which the next subsection removes.

\subsection{Narrowing Helps Most with Uninformative Label Names}
\label{sec:res-relabel}

Narrowing helps most when the label names mean nothing.
On all six datasets we replace every class name by a nonsense word, the same for all seeds, which leaves the base classifier untouched and forces the LLM to learn the labels from the examples (Table~\ref{tab:relabel}, Appendix~\ref{app:relabel}).
With few examples few-shot prompting collapses, and CICLe's advantage on Yahoo Answers grows to $+6.5$ [$+6.0$, $+7.1$] points with Fixed retrieval, the largest gain we measure (Table~\ref{tab:relabel}, Yahoo Answers rows). % PENDING: MASSIVE renaming
On SST-5 the advantage roughly doubles, and on the datasets whose names were weak hints to begin with it stays close to the original (Table~\ref{tab:relabel}).
The candidate list without examples is useless under nonsense names (Table~\ref{tab:relabel}, $k = 0$ rows), so what CICLe adds here is examples from the right classes.
A calibrated set also beats uncalibrated sets of the same size more clearly than under real names (Table~\ref{tab:narrowing}, renamed rows), with one exception on renamed Ohsumed, where the much smaller marginal sets win by three points.

Whether such gains justify an LLM at all is the next question.

\subsection{When the LLM Pipeline Is Worth It}
\label{sec:res-baselines}

An LLM pipeline pays when the labelled pool is skewed or small, and a fine-tuned encoder wins when it is balanced and plentiful.
Table~\ref{tab:main_supervised} puts the fixed configuration, Per-Class CICLe at $k = 4$, next to the base classifier and two fine-tuned encoders, and Figure~\ref{fig:main_poolsize} varies the size of the labelled pool.
With 1,600 balanced labels RoBERTa-base beats every 3B to 8B pipeline on SST-5, SemEval-18, GoEmotions and Ohsumed, and on Yahoo Answers the logistic regression that CICLe narrows with beats both on its own (Table~\ref{tab:main_supervised}, 1$\times$ rows). % PENDING: MASSIVE supervised baselines against the pipelines
The qualification is model size, since Qwen2.5-32B with CICLe overtakes the encoders on Yahoo Answers and SST-5 but not on the two many-class datasets (Table~\ref{tab:large}, Appendix~\ref{app:large}).

At 100 times imbalance the order flips.
Both encoders lose about 20 points on Yahoo Answers while the pipeline loses seven (Table~\ref{tab:main_supervised}, 100$\times$ rows), because retrieval and the LLM's knowledge of the labels carry the rare classes.
The pool-size sweep agrees (Figure~\ref{fig:main_poolsize}).
The pipelines are nearly flat between 250 and 2,000 labels, while RoBERTa-base climbs with the pool and overtakes them on SST-5 between 500 and 1,000 labels (Table~\ref{tab:pool}, Appendix~\ref{app:pool}). % PENDING (rerun): RoBERTa-large at the smallest pools
What remains is how tight the narrowing should be.

\subsection{Choosing $\alpha$ and the Components}
\label{sec:res-alpha}

The miscoverage level should follow the strength of the base classifier.
Figure~\ref{fig:alpha} and Table~\ref{tab:ablation} (Appendix~\ref{app:ablations}) vary $\alpha$, the embedding and the classifier with two Llama models.
Loosening $\alpha$ from 0.05 to 0.20 on Yahoo Answers, where the classifier is strong, turns a null difference into a gain of $+3.6$ [$+2.7$, $+4.5$] points while a third of the inputs never reach the LLM, and it also helps where the sets are otherwise large, at the price of the coverage guarantee.
