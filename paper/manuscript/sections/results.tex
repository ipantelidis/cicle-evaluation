\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All numbers are at the reference setting and averaged over the six small models and three seeds unless stated.
$\Delta$ is CICLe minus an alternative in macro-F1 points with its 95\% bootstrap interval, over 72 paired cells for the main grid and 36 where $k \in \{1, 4\}$ unless stated.

\subsection{The Protocol Decides the Conclusion}
\label{sec:res-protocol}

Under the original prompts, which outputs parse decides which models appear to benefit.
We reran the pilot's protocol for the six models on seed 42 with Fixed retrieval at $k \in \{1, 4\}$ against the corrected one on the same test sample (Table~\ref{tab:legacy}).
Without a label list, 34 to 65\% of the few-shot outputs of Ministral-3B, Qwen2.5-3B and Mistral-7B matched no label on Yahoo Answers, SemEval-18, GoEmotions and Ohsumed, against 0 to 21\% under the corrected protocol, and macro-F1 rises accordingly.
The pilot's two clearest negative results, Ministral-3B and Qwen2.5-7B on Yahoo Answers, are not reproduced over three seeds, $-0.17$ [$-1.09$, $+0.77$] and $+0.05$ [$-0.44$, $+0.54$] (Table~\ref{tab:per_model}, 12 pairs).
Per-Class CICLe never significantly hurts any model, and Fixed CICLe hurts two on Yahoo Answers only, Llama-3.2-3B ($-2.33$ [$-3.13$, $-1.53$]) and Mistral-7B ($-0.69$ [$-1.31$, $-0.06$]).
Residual invalid outputs (Appendix~\ref{app:instances}) count as errors throughout.

\subsection{Narrowing the Label Set Helps a Little, and Reliably}
\label{sec:res-gain}

With wording, pool and seeds held equal, narrowing beats few-shot prompting by a small margin in nine of ten dataset and retrieval cells (Table~\ref{tab:main}, Figure~\ref{fig:tokens}).
The gain is $+1.40$ [$+1.04$, $+1.76$] with Per-Class retrieval on Yahoo Answers, $+1.77$ [$+1.14$, $+2.39$] and $+1.07$ [$+0.42$, $+1.71$] on SST-5, $+0.52$ and $+0.46$ on SemEval-18, $+0.59$ and $+0.92$ on GoEmotions and $+1.15$ [$+0.54$, $+1.87$] Fixed on Ohsumed.
The exception is Yahoo Answers with Fixed retrieval, $-0.19$ [$-0.57$, $+0.20$], where the set keeps 5.7 of 10 classes and one retrieved example already carries the label.
The effects are small and do not grow with $k$ (Appendix~\ref{app:full}).
The one large gain is Ohsumed with Per-Class retrieval, $+5.21$ [$+4.43$, $+6.08$] at $k = 1$ (18 pairs).
A Per-Class few-shot prompt there holds 23 abstracts and 7,500 tokens and scores below a single-abstract prompt (44.6 against 46.7). CICLe halves it to 12.3 abstracts and reaches 49.8, which is context dilution repaired by a smaller example budget.

The list alone is worth about one example.
With the candidate set and no examples ($k = 0$), CICLe scores 0.8 to 4.8 points above zero-shot prompting on every dataset, and on Yahoo Answers, SST-5 and GoEmotions it matches or beats few-shot prompting with one Fixed example.
The second effect is on the prompt.
Per-Class retrieval places $k$ examples per admissible class, so at equal $k$ Per-Class CICLe uses 28 to 38\% fewer tokens than Per-Class few-shot prompting on Yahoo Answers (1,378 against 2,166 at $k = 4$), 11 to 21\% on the other short-text datasets and 45\% on Ohsumed, at the same or higher macro-F1 (Figure~\ref{fig:tokens}, filled markers).
Fixed retrieval is cheaper still, and on GoEmotions it also scores higher (26.6 against 25.9 averaged over $k$) at a sixth of the tokens, because 22 admissible classes times $k$ examples dilute the prompt.

\subsection{How You Narrow Matters}
\label{sec:res-narrowing}

Narrowing could help simply because a shorter list is easier to choose from, in which case any rule that produces sets of the same size would do.
Figure~\ref{fig:imbalance} and Table~\ref{tab:narrowing} test this with top-$m$, probability-mass and marginal conformal sets built from the same classifier at the same mean size.
On the two balanced datasets the rules are interchangeable.
On SST-5 and SemEval-18 no difference between CICLe and top-$m$, probability mass or marginal CP exceeds 1.1 points, and most are not significant (Table~\ref{tab:narrowing}). % PENDING: marginal CP on SemEval-18
On the naturally long-tailed datasets, calibrating per class is worth one to three points.
GoEmotions gives $+2.51$ [$+1.49$, $+3.23$] over top-$m$, $+2.26$ over probability mass and $+1.92$ over marginal CP with Fixed retrieval ($+2.54$, $+1.91$ and $+1.28$ Per-Class), and Ohsumed $+1.08$, $+1.52$ and $+2.88$ [$+1.86$, $+4.08$].
All rules cover 95 to 99\% of gold labels overall, but the lowest per-class coverage is 74\% on GoEmotions and 85\% on Ohsumed under CICLe and 0\% under each alternative (Table~\ref{tab:set_stats}).

\input{figures/fig_imbalance}

The controlled experiment pins the cause on the labelled pool.
With the test sample fixed and the training sample made 10 or 100 times long-tailed, the conformal sets keep their coverage, 95.0, 94.9 and 96.7\% on Yahoo Answers and 95.4, 96.5 and 96.0\% on SST-5.
Top-$m$ sets of the same size fall to 81.6 and 88.4\% at 100 times, probability-mass sets to 74.2 and 60.0\%, and marginal sets to 64.8 and 51.9\% while shrinking to 3.8 and 2.3 classes, because the shared threshold is set by the confident head classes (Figure~\ref{fig:imbalance}b).
The missing coverage is all on the tail.
At 100 times the rarest Yahoo Answers class is in the CICLe set for 98\% of its instances, in the top-$m$ set for 9\%, the probability-mass set for 1\% and the marginal set for 0\% (Figure~\ref{fig:imbalance}c), and per-class F1 collapses on the tail for the three alternatives only (Figure~\ref{fig:imbalance}d).
An LLM cannot recover a label it is not offered.
At 100 times with Per-Class retrieval, CICLe minus top-$m$ is $+7.44$ [$+6.72$, $+8.17$] on Yahoo Answers and $+4.21$ [$+3.37$, $+5.01$] on SST-5, minus probability mass $+9.44$ and $+10.70$, and minus marginal CP $+11.94$ [$+10.98$, $+12.89$] and $+12.92$ [$+11.72$, $+14.10$] (Fixed in Appendix~\ref{app:narrowing}).
At 10 times the gaps are $+0.6$ to $+4.0$ points.

An oracle set that always contains the gold label beats few-shot prompting by $+8.39$ [$+7.68$, $+9.13$] on balanced Yahoo Answers and $+8.73$ on SST-5 (Per-Class), of which CICLe realises $+1.32$ and $+1.14$, and it is 2.4 and 1.3 points above CICLe on GoEmotions and SemEval-18. On balanced data the bottleneck is ranking, not coverage.

Two things do not happen.
CICLe's margin over few-shot prompting does not grow with imbalance.
With Per-Class retrieval it is $+1.32$, $+1.09$ and $+0.89$ at 1, 10 and 100 times on Yahoo Answers and $+1.14$, $+1.82$ and $+1.16$ on SST-5, all significant.
Few-shot prompting lists every label and cannot lose coverage. What imbalance predicts is the gap between narrowing rules.
Second, narrowing is not free of harm.
Over 18,000 instance pairs at $k = 4$ (Table~\ref{tab:fixes}), Per-Class CICLe fixes 4.7\% of Yahoo Answers instances and breaks 3.4\%.
Among the 5.0\% whose gold label the set misses, it fixes none and breaks 4.1\% that few-shot prompting had right, and on SST-5 that share is 35.6\%.
The $1 - \alpha$ coverage is a direct bound on this damage, which is the argument for calibrating per class.

\subsection{Uninformative Label Names}
\label{sec:res-relabel}

Few-shot prompting with informative names can succeed without reading the examples, since the word Sports is a strong hint.
Renaming every class to a nonsense word, identically for all seeds and on all five datasets, removes that hint while keeping the task (Table~\ref{tab:relabel}).
With one Fixed example on Yahoo Answers, few-shot prompting scores 25.3 against 54.8 with the original names, while CICLe keeps 34.3.
The candidate list without examples is useless when its words mean nothing ($k = 0$ falls from 56.3 to 18.9 on Yahoo Answers), so what CICLe adds here is examples from the right classes.
Its advantage over few-shot prompting is the largest we measure, $+6.52$ [$+5.96$, $+7.09$] Fixed and $+5.62$ [$+5.00$, $+6.25$] Per-Class on Yahoo Answers and $+3.75$ and $+2.10$ on SST-5, against $-0.35$ (n.s.), $+1.32$, $+2.05$ and $+1.14$ with the original names.
On SemEval-18 ($+0.79$, $+0.72$), GoEmotions ($+0.66$, $+0.98$) and Ohsumed ($+1.00$) the gain is close to the original one, as the names were weak hints to begin with.
On renamed Yahoo Answers CICLe also beats top-$m$ and probability-mass sets of the same size by 2.6 and 4.5 to 5.0 points (Table~\ref{tab:narrowing}), while on renamed Ohsumed the smaller marginal sets (4.8 against 12.3 labels) beat it by $3.33$ [$2.05$, $4.21$] points.

\subsection{When the LLM Pipeline Is Worth It}
\label{sec:res-baselines}

Table~\ref{tab:baselines} (Appendix~\ref{app:pool}) puts the pipelines next to models that never call an LLM.
The fixed configuration, Per-Class retrieval at $k = 4$, is the main number, and the best configuration per column, which is selected on the test set, is secondary.
With 1,600 balanced labels a fine-tuned RoBERTa-base beats CICLe at the fixed configuration on four of five datasets (51.2 against 47.5 on SST-5, 24.5 against 15.7 on SemEval-18, 37.0 against 26.0 on GoEmotions, 55.4 against 51.7 on Ohsumed).
On Yahoo Answers the logistic regression that CICLe narrows with scores 65.4 on its own, above every 3B to 8B pipeline (62.7, best 63.8) and above RoBERTa-base (61.8).
The qualification is model size.
Qwen2.5-32B with CICLe reaches 66.9 on Yahoo Answers and 55.9 on SST-5 (Appendix~\ref{app:large}), above both encoders and the logistic regression, but stays below RoBERTa-base on SemEval-18 (20.2) and GoEmotions (33.6).
RoBERTa-large beats RoBERTa-base on Yahoo Answers and Ohsumed (64.6 and 66.1) and loses elsewhere under our recipe. % PENDING (rerun): RoBERTa-large is being retrained with a lower learning rate because two seeds collapsed; update these two numbers and Tables 4 and P

The order flips when the labelled pool is skewed.
At 100 times imbalance RoBERTa-base falls to 41.2 on Yahoo Answers and 31.9 on SST-5 and the logistic regression to 31.4 and 12.6, while CICLe loses 7.3 and 5.6 points (55.4 and 41.9) with few-shot prompting close behind (54.6 and 40.8).
The pool-size sweep agrees (Figure~\ref{fig:pool} and Table~\ref{tab:pool} in Appendix~\ref{app:pool}).
The pipelines are nearly flat between 250 and 2,000 labels, 60.5 to 62.7 on Yahoo Answers and 45.0 to 47.5 on SST-5, while RoBERTa-base climbs from 52.2 to 61.8 and from 38.8 to 51.2.
On SST-5 the encoder overtakes the pipelines between 500 and 1,000 labels. On Yahoo Answers it never does, but the logistic regression does at 1,000.
Narrowing itself needs calibration data.
With 250 labels, 50 of them for calibration, CICLe's coverage is 92\% on SST-5 and its Per-Class margin is $-0.63$ [$-1.33$, $+0.13$], turning positive at 500 and 1,000 labels ($+0.60$ and $+0.83$ [$+0.20$, $+1.47$]). % CHECK: from paper/reference/analysis_2026-10-08_final.txt (B=2000); NUMBERS.md has no pool-size deltas
With a plentiful balanced pool, fine-tune an encoder, or use the base classifier alone when it is strong.
With a skewed or small pool, use an in-context pipeline, and narrow it with class-conditional conformal sets once a few hundred calibration examples are available.

\subsection{Choosing $\alpha$, Components and Models}
\label{sec:res-alpha}

$\alpha$ sets the coverage of the candidate set, its size and the share of instances the classifier answers alone (Figure~\ref{fig:alpha} and Table~\ref{tab:ablation} in Appendix~\ref{app:ablations}, two Llama models, 24 cells).
Loosening it from 0.05 to 0.20 on Yahoo Answers turns $-0.37$ (n.s.) against few-shot prompting into $+3.57$ [$+2.67$, $+4.48$] while 35\% of the instances never reach the LLM.
On GoEmotions and SemEval-18, where sets at 0.05 hold 22 of 28 and 17 of 20 classes, it adds $+3.02$ and $+1.36$, and on SST-5 the gain is flat at $+2.5$ to $+2.8$ for every $\alpha \geq 0.05$.
Among the other components the embedding matters more than the classifier.
Contriever beats MiniLM on SST-5 and GoEmotions ($+3.69$ against $+2.65$ and $+2.46$ against $+0.92$ over few-shot prompting) but not on Yahoo Answers, TF-IDF is weakest everywhere, and an SVM is within 0.4 points of the logistic regression.

The gain does not depend on one prompt.
With a second template on seed 42 (Table~\ref{tab:robust}), CICLe's Per-Class margin is $+1.88$ [$+0.78$, $+3.00$] on SST-5, $+0.71$ on SemEval-18 and $+1.32$ Fixed on Ohsumed, and not significant on Yahoo Answers and GoEmotions.
With random instead of retrieved examples, few-shot prompting loses about 5 points on Yahoo Answers and CICLe's Per-Class margin grows to $+2.26$ [$+1.24$, $+3.29$]. % PENDING: random retrieval on SST-5, SemEval-18 and Ohsumed

Larger models gain less but do not lose (Table~\ref{tab:large}, six pairs per cell).
Mistral-NeMo-12B gains $+4.20$ [$+3.08$, $+5.26$] Fixed and $+4.39$ Per-Class on SST-5 and $+0.2$ to $+0.8$ (n.s.) elsewhere, and Qwen2.5-32B gains $+0.72$ and $+0.94$ on Yahoo Answers, $+1.53$ Fixed on SST-5 and $+0.2$ to $+0.5$ (n.s.) otherwise.
On seed 42, a 3B model with Per-Class CICLe at $k = 4$ beats the 32B model's zero-shot score on Yahoo Answers (61.5 against 60.7) and SST-5 (51.0 against 48.8) and loses on SemEval-18 (14.9 against 15.3) and GoEmotions (24.4 against 32.0). % PENDING: 12B and 32B on Ohsumed
