\subsection{Overall Coverage Hides Unreachable Classes}
\label{sec:res-coverage}

A candidate set can contain the gold label for almost every test instance and still never offer a rare class.
We compare the overall and the per-class coverage of the four narrowing rules on the natural datasets and on labelled pools resampled to a 100-fold skew (Table~\ref{tab:set_stats}, Figure~\ref{fig:main_narrowing}b).
On GoEmotions, top-$m$ sets cover 98.8\% of the test instances, more than CICLe's sets, yet the rarest classes are never in them.
Under a 100-fold skew of the Yahoo Answers pool, the rarest class is in the top-$m$ set for 9\% of its instances and in the marginal conformal set for none (Figure~\ref{fig:main_narrowing}b).
CICLe's sets keep every class, and the lowest coverage of any class, an empirical minimum over classes and seeds, is 89\% on SST-5 at 100 times. % PENDING: class-wise coverage table with Wilson intervals
This minimum is consistent with the guarantee of Section~\ref{sec:background}, which holds in expectation and lets a finite test sample fluctuate below $1-\alpha$, and the rarest classes, with one or two calibration examples, are kept in almost every set by construction.
Whether a lost class also costs macro-F1 once the set size is matched is the central experiment.

\subsection{The Rule Matters Under Skew, Not on Balanced Data}
\label{sec:res-narrowing}

At matched mean set size the narrowing rule makes no measurable difference on balanced data and a large one under skew.
Figure~\ref{fig:main_narrowing}a compares CICLe with top-$m$ sets of the same mean size on Yahoo Answers and SST-5, whose labelled pool is resampled to 10 or 100 times imbalance while the test sample stays fixed, and Table~\ref{tab:narrowing} gives every comparison, including the natural datasets. % PENDING: size-matched probability-mass and marginal rules; MASSIVE
On the balanced pools no difference between CICLe and top-$m$ exceeds one point (Table~\ref{tab:narrowing}, SST-5 and SemEval-18 rows).
Under a 100-fold skew CICLe beats top-$m$ by $+7.4$ [$+6.7$, $+8.2$] points on Yahoo Answers with Per-Class retrieval, and by 3 to 4 points on SST-5 (Table~\ref{tab:narrowing}, 100$\times$ rows).
On the naturally long-tailed GoEmotions the gap is about 2.5 points in both retrieval variants, although top-$m$ covers more test instances overall.
Marginal calibration behaves the same way, level with CICLe on the balanced pools and 12 points behind at 100 times on Yahoo Answers, where its shared threshold is set by the confident head classes, but its sets are smaller than CICLe's, so this comparison also changes the set size. % PENDING: size-matched marginal rule
The effect of the rule is large relative to the gain of narrowing itself, since at the same 100-fold skew CICLe beats few-shot prompting by less than one point on Yahoo Answers (Figure~\ref{fig:main_narrowing}a, grey line).
That gain also does not grow with imbalance, because few-shot prompting lists every label and cannot lose coverage, so imbalance predicts the gap between narrowing rules, not the gap between narrowing and few-shot prompting.
Why a lost class costs this much is the next question.

\subsection{The LLM Does Not Recover a Label Left Out}
\label{sec:res-consequence}

The LLM almost never answers with a label that is missing from the candidate set, so per-class coverage bounds what narrowing can achieve.
We pair every test instance between CICLe and few-shot prompting (Table~\ref{tab:fixes}, Appendix~\ref{app:instances}) and count how often CICLe is right when its set misses the gold label.
Pooled over all datasets and runs, this happens for 0.9\% of such instances, never on Yahoo Answers and Ohsumed, and at most 3.5\% on SST-5, whose ordered sentiment labels the model can still name.
Per-class F1 under skew therefore collapses at exactly the frequency ranks where coverage collapses (Figure~\ref{fig:imbalance}, Appendix~\ref{app:narrowing}), and class-averaged coverage, not overall coverage, tracks macro-F1 across rules and datasets. % PENDING: class-averaged coverage against macro-F1 across rules and dataset variants
The damage is bounded by the $1-\alpha$ coverage, which is the argument for calibrating per class.
Coverage is not the only limit, since an oracle set that always contains the gold label beats few-shot prompting by about 8 points on the balanced pools, of which CICLe realises about one, so on balanced data the remaining room is in ranking (Table~\ref{tab:narrowing}, oracle column).
With these limits in view, the last question is when narrowing pays off at all.

\subsection{When Narrowing Pays Off}
\label{sec:res-gain}

Narrowing gives a small gain over few-shot prompting on short texts with informative labels and a larger one when the label names carry no meaning.
Table~\ref{tab:main} compares zero-shot, few-shot and CICLe prompting on the five datasets, and Figure~\ref{fig:main_tokens} in Appendix~\ref{app:full} plots every configuration against its prompt length. % PENDING: MASSIVE column
CICLe is ahead in nine of ten dataset and retrieval cells, by 0.5 to 1.8 points with instance-level intervals that exclude zero (Table~\ref{tab:main}B). % PENDING: hierarchical intervals and sign counts in Table 2B; MASSIVE
The exception is Fixed retrieval on Yahoo Answers, where one retrieved example already carries the label, and the gains do not grow with $k$ (Appendix~\ref{app:full}).
On Ohsumed with Per-Class retrieval the gain is $+5.2$ [$+4.4$, $+6.1$] points, where a few-shot prompt holds one long abstract per class. % PENDING: one sentence separating narrowing from prompt length, few-shot Fixed k=8 against CICLe Per-Class k=1 on Ohsumed
The candidate list alone, with no examples, is worth about one retrieved example (Table~\ref{tab:main}A, row $k = 0$).


Narrowing also lowers the cost per input, since a narrower candidate set needs fewer Per-Class examples and a singleton set needs no LLM call. % PENDING: expected input tokens per test instance, counting instances answered without an LLM call
The gain is largest when the label names mean nothing.
When every class name is replaced by a nonsense word, which leaves the base classifier untouched, CICLe's advantage over few-shot prompting on Yahoo Answers grows to $+6.5$ [$+6.0$, $+7.1$] points with Fixed retrieval, because few-shot prompting can no longer read the task from the names (Table~\ref{tab:relabel}, Appendix~\ref{app:relabel}). % PENDING: MASSIVE renaming
On the datasets whose names were weak hints to begin with, the gain stays close to the original one.

\subsection{Qualified Secondary Findings}
\label{sec:res-secondary}
\label{sec:res-baselines}
\label{sec:res-alpha}

For the recipes we tested, a fine-tuned encoder beats the 3B to 8B pipelines on a plentiful balanced pool and loses on a skewed or small one.
Table~\ref{tab:main_supervised} puts Per-Class CICLe at $k = 4$ next to the base classifier and two fine-tuned encoders, and Figure~\ref{fig:main_poolsize} in Appendix~\ref{app:pool} varies the pool size.
With 1,600 balanced labels RoBERTa-base beats every 3B to 8B pipeline on four of the five datasets, and on Yahoo Answers the base classifier alone does. % PENDING: MASSIVE
Under a 100-fold skew both encoders lose about 20 points on Yahoo Answers while the pipeline loses seven, and Qwen2.5-32B with CICLe overtakes the encoders on Yahoo Answers and SST-5 (Table~\ref{tab:large}). % PENDING: 12B and 32B on Ohsumed and MASSIVE


Loosening $\alpha$ is dataset-dependent advice.
On Yahoo Answers, where the classifier is strong, moving from 0.05 to 0.20 turns a null difference into $+3.6$ [$+2.7$, $+4.5$] points while a third of the inputs never reach the LLM, but it weakens the coverage guarantee and helps little on SST-5 (Figure~\ref{fig:alpha}, Table~\ref{tab:ablation}).
The gain over few-shot prompting survives a second prompt template and random retrieval on seed 42 (Table~\ref{tab:robust}), and the larger models gain less but do not lose (Table~\ref{tab:large}). % PENDING: random retrieval on SST-5, SemEval-18 and Ohsumed
Best-configuration numbers in the appendix are selected on the test set and are exploratory.
